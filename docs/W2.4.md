# W2.4: Line of sight and direct fire

W2.4 adds the first combat orders to the existing simultaneous WEGO turn. It
deliberately establishes direct fire before fog of war so that LOS and combat
effects can be inspected and tuned with both forces visible.

## Delivered

- Fire is available from a friendly unit's right-click action menu and the
  touch-friendly footer.
- Selecting Fire enters a target mode. Mouse movement or the keyboard cursor
  previews the line to a prospective target; click, Enter, or the footer
  confirms a legal enemy target. Escape, right-click, or Cancel aborts.
- LOS follows the actual 250-meter hex grid and is symmetric in either
  direction.
- Intervening woods and urban terrain block LOS. Terrain elevation and ridges
  can also block LOS, while cultivated ground and concealment in the target
  hex produce an obscured shot instead of an absolute barrier.
- The first wooded hex along a sightline can be visible but obscured. Any
  intervening wooded hex blocks visibility deeper into or through the forest,
  even from higher ground. This conservative hex-scale rule is shared by the
  visibility overlay and direct fire.
- Clear, obscured, blocked, out-of-range, friendly, and unsuitable indirect-fire
  targets receive distinct explanations. A blocked line marks the obstruction.
- Weapon range, hard attack, soft attack, and defence are loaded from the
  external unit-type data already maintained in SQLite and CSV.
- Direct fire resolves during the same WEGO clock as movement. Targets can be
  moving when the shot is evaluated.
- Results apply strength loss, suppression, readiness loss, and morale loss.
  Destroyed counters disappear, stop moving, no longer block movement, and are
  removed from the minimap.
- Fire lines and impact markers remain visible through Review, and the unit
  inspector reports the target, range, LOS quality, status, and result.
- The initial opposing-force logic fires at the nearest legal NATO target or
  advances when it has no shot.

## Player workflow

To inspect LOS without giving an order, select a unit in Planning or Review,
then choose the **LOS** footer button or press **L**. Move the mouse or keyboard
cursor to any hex. All visible hexes are highlighted: green means clear and
amber means visible but obscured. Blocked hexes remain unhighlighted. The
footer reminds players that both green and amber mean visible. Adjacent hexes
are always visible in this hex-center model, although concealing terrain can
make them amber. Higher elevation does not guarantee visibility beyond adjacent
hexes: intervening ridges, woods, and buildings can still obstruct the ray. The
inspection line turns red when blocked; an X marks the obstruction. Visibility
is calculated across the whole map and cached while inspecting, so panning and
zooming retain the overlay. The footer reports the range and blocking
reason. This tool is independent of weapon range and does not alter confirmed
orders. Select Done or press Escape/L to close it.

Wheel zoom reuses fixed-resolution terrain chunks instead of discarding and
redrawing the terrain at every zoom level. Each full wheel notch changes zoom
by 20%, and multiple-notch wheel events retain their magnitude.

The selected unit's movement route is highlighted in yellow and drawn above
other routes. Traffic bypasses are limited to local reconnection to the original
route and preserve intermediate waypoints. An occupied waypoint remains a hold.

1. Select a NATO unit, then choose Fire from the footer or right-click menu.
2. Move the cursor to an enemy counter. The line and footer explain whether the
   shot is clear, obscured, blocked, or beyond the unit's range.
3. Click the target or press Enter to plan the shot.
4. Plan other units, then select Execute.
5. Observe simultaneous movement and fire and inspect the results in Review.

Movement and direct fire are mutually exclusive orders for a unit in this
milestone. Indirect-fire units are identified but cannot yet use their database
range as direct fire.

## Deferred

- Weather and daylight visibility limits, with later sensor/optics distinctions.

- Fog of war, difficulty levels, spotting, sound contacts, and uncertain
  contact markers (W2.5).
- Assault, retreat, surrender, and close-combat morale reactions (W2.6).
- Indirect artillery, ammunition, smoke, and air defence.
- Detailed weapon-by-weapon penetration, facing, and range bands. W2.4 uses the
  existing platoon-level combat ratings as its tunable first model.
